## Implemented boundary

- Eight public indexed commitments per round. Each is a hash of the round, side, and random 32-byte salt. The circuit inputs for side and salt are private; the commitment, count, transaction, and timing are public.
- Submission asserts the opening file's expected round equals the contract's current round, so a saved opening cannot silently land in a later round.
- A resolver supplies all eight openings to one proof. The contract checks every opening and discloses only the winning side, then writes a 70/30 quote-side target and reapportions internal NIGHT redemption capacity in the same call. A tie preserves the previous target. Resolution moves no assets outside contract custody.
- Anyone can submit multiple positions before a public round close. The browser chooses the first close seven days from its deployment request; the contract advances later close times by exactly 604,800 seconds. The contract uses [Compact block-time predicates](https://docs.midnight.network/compact/standard-library/exports) to reject later commitments and early/late resolution. There is no unique-human check. Opening files are not recoverable from the chain.
- A full round may resolve from close until one day later. After that, anyone may expire an unresolved round, including a partially filled one, without changing the allocation target; expiry reapplies that target to internal reserves. This recovery path discloses no opening. The first close is a public constructor argument chosen by the deployer, not independently derived from block time; users should check it before participating. The [Midnight security guide](https://docs.midnight.network/guides/security-best-practices) documents these Unix-second predicates and the absence of a raw block-time accessor.
- The resolver and its proof server see every opening. The dedicated proof server is bound to loopback port 6301. [Midnight's security guide](https://docs.midnight.network/guides/security-best-practices) treats public ledger writes and transaction timing as observable and prover-supplied values as untrusted for authentication.
- Resolution, expiry, and reserve restoration append an on-chain treasury action with its round, type, winner, target, reserves, and fee pool. The browser reads the ten newest records from public contract state. These records contain no transaction ID or timestamp. A no-op restoration is rejected. Saved opening files can be previewed locally; a finalized commitment creates a locally saved public receipt for the connected wallet and contract. The file alone proves no Preprod participation.

The history change adds no private witness, disclosure, or participant identifier: it copies already-public post-action fields into an append-only ledger map. The local preview parses an opening in the browser, displays only its round/side/contract metadata, does not send the file, and clears that preview on local disconnect. Locally saved receipts contain only public round, block, and transaction ID; they remain in this browser after disconnect but are shown again only after connecting the same wallet and contract. No opening side or salt is stored. This does not conceal wallet transaction timing or protect an opening from a compromised device, resolver, or proof server.

## Locally compiled economic boundary

The [Fantastical Factory reference](https://docs.fantasticalfactory.com/) informs the three-token, asymmetric-friction loop; its Solana mechanics are not ported as Midnight features. [Midnight's unshielded token guide](https://docs.midnight.network/tokens/unshielded-token) documents native issuance/transfers. The pinned compiler accepts fixed-supply contract-custodied genesis for Quote A, Quote B, and KAI; NIGHT/quote buy and sell calls; public basis-point fees; and a conditional KAI distribution coupled to the fee. Twelve local contract tests pass, including deadline, atomic reserve allocation, exact fee, reserve invariants, and treasury history. The local proof server checked and generated proofs for all eight exported circuits with synthetic inputs after this change. The tests explicitly supply settled unshielded balances between circuit calls; they do not model a full Preprod transaction or Lace balancing.

Unshielded minting has a [reported Compact ledger-partitioning failure](https://github.com/LFDT-Minokawa/compact/issues/235) when combined with fallible operations. Genesis contains no assert and later calls cannot inflate supply, but no transaction has been submitted and finalized on Preprod. A real wallet transaction is required before claiming issuance or trade runtime success. Direct wallet-to-wallet transfers bypass Kairos routes and fees. KAI is a fixed-supply trade incentive, not a claim on fees or NIGHT; the public fee pool has no withdrawal route.

The former product's staked-token yield and stake-weighted vote have not been ported. The current public asset primitives could hold KAI, but they do not provide an authenticated unstake or fee-claim path by themselves. In particular, `ownPublicKey()` is not a safe caller-authentication shortcut, and a public stake amount next to a small private round could weaken position privacy. A locked deposit without a recoverable owner-controlled withdrawal and funded reward allocation would be misleading. The supported private commitment route therefore remains free to enter, and KAI is delivered only as the documented trade incentive. This is a design boundary, not a claim that Midnight can never support staking.

Resolution or expiry now mathematically constrains the internal split of NIGHT redemption capacity to the target percentage in the same transaction. The caller supplies a public candidate allocation computed from public reserves; the circuit proves it is the exact floor division result, so an incorrect candidate cannot steer funds. A separate public repair call remains for later trades that change the split. The next round cannot start until the current split matches the target. This affects which side can redeem; it does not move external liquidity or guarantee a side can redeem after capacity moves away. No supported external liquidity venue/counterparty path was verified. Deadlines are enforced, but autonomous transaction submission is not implemented; an operator still submits resolution or expiry and round-advance calls. If an operator misses multiple weeks, overdue rounds require explicit expiry and advance calls.

## Upgrade boundaries

Keep market participation, resolution, policy calculation, and execution as distinct code boundaries even when internal allocation shares a transaction with resolution. Before expanding economic claims, verify mint, trade, reserve, and KAI transactions on Preprod with their public balances. A treasury executor must demonstrate a real venue and authenticated authority before moving external liquidity. Keep the UI and README scoped to the behavior actually verified at each stage.
